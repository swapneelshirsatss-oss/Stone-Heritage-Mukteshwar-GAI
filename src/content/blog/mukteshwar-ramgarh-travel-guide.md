# Complete Travel Guide to Mukteshwar and Ramgarh (2026) — Where to Stay, What to See, and How to Get There

There is a stretch of road in Nainital district that most Delhi NCR travellers drive past without stopping. It begins at Bhowali, climbs steadily through oak and rhododendron forest, passes through the quiet fruit orchards of Ramgarh, and ends at the dramatic cliff-edge temple town of Mukteshwar — where the Himalayan range unfolds in front of you at 2,286 metres like a wall of white.

This corridor — the Bhowali-Ramgarh-Mukteshwar Road — is one of the most underrated mountain drives in all of Uttarakhand. And the two destinations at its heart, Mukteshwar and Ramgarh, are quietly becoming the preferred alternatives for experienced Kumaon travellers who have outgrown the crowds of Nainital.

This guide covers everything you need to plan your trip — what to see, where to stay (from heritage homestays to boutique resorts), when to go, and how to get there from Delhi, Gurugram, and Noida.

---

## Where Are Mukteshwar and Ramgarh?

Mukteshwar is a small hill town in Nainital district, Uttarakhand, located at approximately 2,286 metres above sea level — around 51 km from Nainital and 75 km from Haldwani. It is named after the ancient Mukteshwar Dham Shiva temple that perches dramatically on a rocky cliff overlooking the Himalayan range.

Ramgarh is a quieter village approximately 25 km before Mukteshwar on the same road — lower in altitude (around 1,780 metres) but arguably more scenic, sitting in the middle of what is known as the Fruit Bowl of Kumaon. Ramgarh's apple, peach, plum, and apricot orchards have made it famous among travellers who want orchard walks, pine forest trails, and genuine mountain quiet without the tourist activity of Mukteshwar market.

Together, the Bhowali-Ramgarh-Mukteshwar corridor offers a complete Kumaon mountain experience — spiritual sites, nature treks, Himalayan views, and orchard culture — all within a 25 km stretch of one of India's most beautiful mountain roads.

## Best Time to Visit Mukteshwar and Ramgarh

Every season on the Bhowali-Ramgarh-Mukteshwar Road offers a completely different experience. There is genuinely no bad time to visit — only different versions of what this corridor offers.

### Summer — March to June
The most popular season for families and first-time visitors. Temperatures range from 12 to 24 degrees Celsius. The Ramgarh orchards are in full blossom from March to May — pink and white peach blossoms covering the hillsides in one of Uttarakhand's most beautiful natural spectacles. Clear skies mean excellent Himalayan views throughout the day.

Best for: families, orchard walks, Nanda Devi views, outdoor activities

### Monsoon — July to September
The corridor transforms into vivid green — forests deepen, waterfalls emerge, and the Bhalu Gaad trail becomes lush and dramatic. Fog and mist roll through Mukteshwar in the evenings. Road conditions should be checked before travel during heavy rain periods.

Best for: photographers, nature lovers, budget travellers, misty mountain aesthetic

### Winter — October to February
The most atmospheric season — temperatures drop to 2–8 degrees Celsius, snowfall dusts the Himalayan peaks from November onwards, and the Kumaon sky is at its clearest with the Nanda Devi range visible in sharp detail. Mukteshwar town becomes extremely quiet. Bonfire evenings and fireplace rooms are at their most rewarding.

Best for: couples, honeymooners, returning travellers, those seeking maximum Himalayan view clarity

### Festival Season — October and November
Nanda Devi Mela at Mukteshwar Dham and local Kumaoni festivals bring the community alive. A wonderful time to experience authentic Kumaoni culture alongside the natural beauty of the corridor.

## What to See and Do — Top Experiences in Mukteshwar and Ramgarh

![Top experiences in Mukteshwar and Ramgarh including Mukteshwar Dham Temple, Chauli Ki Jali, Bhalu Gaad Waterfall, Kainchi Dham, Ramgarh Orchards, and bird watching](/Blog-images/What-to-See-and-Do-Top-Experiences-in-Mukteshwar-and-Ramgarh.webp)

### 1. Mukteshwar Dham Temple

The defining experience of any visit to Mukteshwar — a 350-year-old Shiva temple perched at 2,286 metres on a rocky summit with a sheer cliff drop on one side and unobstructed Himalayan panoramas on the other. Nanda Devi, Trishul, and Panchachuli are all visible from the temple courtyard on clear mornings.

The best time to visit is early morning — arrive by 7 AM before the day-tripper crowds from Nainital arrive, and you will have the temple and the view almost entirely to yourself.

**Distance from Ramgarh:** 25 km (45 minutes)
**Distance from Mukteshwar market:** 3 km (10 minutes)

### 2. Chauli Ki Jali — Cliffs, Paragliding and Sunset Views

Immediately adjacent to Mukteshwar Dham temple, the Chauli Ki Jali cliff formations are a legendary spot for anyone who visits the corridor. The name translates loosely to "the witch's net" — a dramatic rocky overhang where adventurous visitors can lean out over the cliff edge and look straight down.

For those who prefer their adventure more structured, certified paragliding operators launch from Chauli Ki Jali — the valley views from altitude are extraordinary. Rock climbing routes of varying difficulty are also available with local guide operators.

The cliff faces west — making Chauli Ki Jali the finest sunset viewpoint in the entire Bhowali-Ramgarh-Mukteshwar corridor. Arrive by 5 PM on clear days.

**Distance from Ramgarh:** 25 km (45 minutes)

### 3. Bhalu Gaad Waterfall Trek

One of the finest short treks accessible from the Ramgarh-Mukteshwar corridor — and one of the least crowded. A 2 km walk through three distinct forest zones (deodar, oak scrub, and riparian pine) leads to a hidden natural waterfall and plunge pool set in a rocky amphitheatre.

The trail passes along a stream corridor where Himalayan bulbuls, kalij pheasants, and forest eagles are regularly spotted — making it an excellent Himalayan bird-watching walk in addition to a nature trek. Suitable for families, children, and first-time trekkers. No technical equipment required.

**Distance from Ramgarh:** 12 km (20 minutes drive, then 2 km on foot)
**Best time:** Morning, October to June

### 4. Kainchi Dham — Neem Karoli Baba Ashram

One of the most spiritually significant destinations in all of Uttarakhand — and increasingly one of the most internationally recognised. The Kainchi Dham Ashram, dedicated to Neem Karoli Baba (also known as Maharaj-ji), draws devotees, seekers, and curious travellers from across India and internationally.

The ashram sits in a beautifully tranquil valley along the Kosi river, about 22 km from Ramgarh and 17 km from Bhowali. Arrive before 9 AM for the morning aarti and before the day's main visitor flow. The atmosphere at peak hours can be overwhelming for those seeking quiet spiritual experience — early morning visits are deeply recommended.

**Distance from Ramgarh:** 22 km (45 minutes)

### 5. Ramgarh Fruit Orchards — The Fruit Bowl of Kumaon

Ramgarh's orchard culture is something no other destination in Uttarakhand offers in quite the same way. The entire valley hillside is planted with apple, peach, plum, and apricot trees — many of them decades old, maintained by local farming families who have cultivated these orchards since the British colonial era.

The blossom season (March to May) is spectacular — pink peach blossoms and white apple blossom cover the slopes. Harvest season (August to October) offers a completely different experience — fruit picking, jam making, and the scent of ripe orchards on the mountain air.

The best way to experience the orchards is to stay within them — several properties in Ramgarh sit directly in active working orchards and offer guided orchard walks as part of the stay experience.

### 6. Himalayan Bird Watching

The Bhowali-Ramgarh-Mukteshwar corridor is one of the finest accessible bird-watching locations in the Kumaon Himalayas. Species regularly spotted along the road and forest trails include the Himalayan bulbul, kalij pheasant, rufous-bellied niltava, bar-tailed treecreeper, Eurasian jay, and multiple species of Himalayan eagle and hawk.

The stream corridor along the Bhalu Gaad trail is the single most productive bird-watching site for the area. Early morning (6–8 AM) is peak activity time.

## How to Reach Mukteshwar and Ramgarh

### By Road from Delhi/NCR

The Bhowali-Ramgarh-Mukteshwar Road is one of the most scenic mountain drives accessible from Delhi — and one of the most underrated road trips in north India.

**Route:** NH9 (Delhi) → Gajraula → Moradabad → Rampur → Rudrapur → Haldwani → Bhowali → Ramgarh → Malla Ramgarh → Mukteshwar

**Distance from Delhi:** approximately 330 km to Mukteshwar, 305 km to Ramgarh
**Drive time:** 7–9 hours depending on traffic at Delhi exit and Haldwani entry

**From Gurugram:** Join NH48 → NH9 at Gurgaon-Delhi border, then follow the Delhi route above
**From Noida:** Exit via NH9 directly — slightly shorter entry to the expressway corridor
**From Faridabad:** Join NH19 → NH9 via Palwal — add approximately 45 minutes to Delhi timings

**Pro tip:** Leave Delhi or NCR by 4–5 AM to clear the city and hit Haldwani before the main morning rush. The mountain section from Bhowali onwards (approximately 2 hours of the drive) is the most scenic and requires daylight — timing departure to arrive at Bhowali around 10–11 AM is ideal.

### By Train

**Nearest railhead:** Kathgodam Railway Station — approximately 40 minutes from Ramgarh and 55 minutes from Mukteshwar by road.

**Key trains from Delhi:**
- Ranikhet Express (overnight) — Delhi to Kathgodam, departs late evening
- Kathgodam Shatabdi — morning departure, arrives early afternoon

From Kathgodam, pre-arranged cab transfers are available directly from most properties in Ramgarh and Mukteshwar. Book the transfer in advance when making your accommodation reservation.

### By Air

**Nearest airport:** Pantnagar Airport — approximately 60 minutes from Ramgarh and 75 minutes from Mukteshwar by road.

Flight connections from Delhi (45 minutes), Mumbai, and Bengaluru are available. Most properties can arrange cab transfers from Pantnagar on prior request.

## Practical Travel Tips

**Mobile network:** Jio and Airtel have the strongest signal on this corridor. BSNL has patchy coverage. Most properties offer Wi-Fi but carry a backup data SIM.

**Weather:** Carry layers regardless of season — temperatures drop significantly after sunset even in summer. Winter visits require proper warm clothing — temperatures at Mukteshwar regularly touch 0 degrees Celsius in December and January.

**Road conditions:** The Bhowali-Ramgarh-Mukteshwar Road is a well-maintained state highway. Narrow sections exist between Ramgarh and Mukteshwar — drive carefully, especially around blind curves.

**Cash:** Carry cash from Haldwani or Bhowali. ATMs are available in Mukteshwar market but can be unreliable during peak weekends.

**Altitude:** Mukteshwar at 2,286 metres requires minimal acclimatisation for most visitors. Guests with cardiac or respiratory conditions should consult their physician before travel above 1,800 metres.

## Why the Bhowali-Ramgarh-Mukteshwar Corridor Is Worth Your Time

The simplest answer is this: it gives you everything Nainital offers — Himalayan views, colonial hill station character, nature walks, spiritual sites — without the Nainital crowds.

Nainital has become one of the busiest tourist destinations in north India. Peak weekends see the Mall Road impassable, the lake viewpoints shoulder-to-shoulder, and hotel rooms triple their rates. The Bhowali-Ramgarh-Mukteshwar corridor, a mere 40 km from Nainital, is still genuinely quiet on most weekends — a mountain road where you can stop in the middle of the road to photograph a Himalayan bulbul without causing a traffic jam.

The travellers who discover this corridor once rarely go back to Nainital. They come back here instead.
